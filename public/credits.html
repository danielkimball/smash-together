Smash Portal is a free browser frontend, multiplayer lobby and relay. Each player supplies their own original US Super Smash Bros. ROM. The opening, title and attract demonstration are audiovisual recordings from the host’s cartridge. These recordings cannot be played as a game. Live gameplay uses the player’s verified copy; no ROM is included in the static website or source archive.

The emulator

waveplate/M64Wasm adds deterministic netplay and save-state exports to Neil Barkhina’s N64Wasm. This release uses commit f6b5d6cc31821ec501a94aa761eae022d08f5a9d. The wrapper has an MIT license.

The engine incorporates Mupen64Plus, libretro/ParaLLEl-N64, graphics and RSP plugins, SDL, Emscripten, zlib, and other libraries. Copyright and license notices are retained alongside their source. See the Mupen64Plus GPL and gles2n64 LGPL notices.

The small things

The server uses ws (MIT). The emulator overlay uses Abel by MADType (SIL Open Font License), replacing the fonts in the original runtime asset archive. The website uses locally served Josefin Sans, Instrument Sans and Orbitron, each under the SIL Open Font License. The Smash Portal logo and brand direction were supplied by Dan. Game footage and branding retain their respective ownership; the code license does not relicense them.

Internet sharing uses a free Cloudflare Quick Tunnel from your computer. Its temporary address changes when it restarts. Direct player connections use WebRTC; a relay carries traffic when a direct connection is unavailable.

Your files and your connection

ROM files are read locally and remembered in this browser’s storage. The server does not accept ROM uploads. Multiplayer exchanges controller inputs, game-state checks and compressed save states; the relay forwards them without storing matches. Public rooms appear in the room browser. Invite-only rooms stay hidden; their links act as invitation keys.

There are no accounts, analytics, ads or subscriptions in the app. Local saves can be exported and imported. Forget saved ROM removes the browser’s stored copy. Hardware controller support depends on the browser and your adapter’s Gamepad API support; Chrome or Edge on a computer is recommended.
